# Khloe: product brief

*Source material for marketing. Every statement below reflects what the app does today.*

## 1. What Khloe is
Khloe is an online booking page for people who sell their time. Before a client picks a time, it asks them a few questions, steers them to the right service, then handles the booking, the calendar, the reminders and, if wanted, the payment.

## 2. Who it's for
- **B2B.** The paying customer is the business. Its clients book for free and never need an account.
- **Users:** independent professionals and small studios who sell sessions, such as coaches, consultants, photographers, wedding planners and therapists. The examples in the app and onboarding point this way: discovery calls, portrait sessions, multi-session programmes.
- **Accounts:** one business per account, created through self-serve sign-up. Owner, admin and member roles exist in the data model, but there is no way yet to invite a team member from the app.

## 3. Core features
- **Booking page:** each business gets its own address, a link that can open on one service, a logo and brand colour, and a "Back to your website" link.
- **On your own website:** a guided setup detects the website tool (Squarespace, Wix, WordPress, Webflow, Shopify, Framer), shows only that tool's steps and can email them to whoever runs the site. Once the code is up, Khloe shows whether it's working there.
- **Questions before booking:** yes/no, multiple-choice or free-text questions. Answers can send someone to the right service or to another outcome, such as a message or a link elsewhere.
- **Unfinished enquiries are kept:** the client's email is asked first, so the business can see and follow up people who stopped before booking.
- **AI assistance:** drafts the questions from a short description of the work, and writes summaries of the business's reports. Both are limited each month.
- **Services:** length, price, buffers, location (online, in person or phone), single sessions or multi-session programmes, and services for existing clients only.
- **Availability:** weekly hours, date exceptions, time off, blocked times, minimum notice and how far ahead clients can book.
- **Google Calendar:** busy times are hidden from the booking page, each booking creates an event with a Google Meet link, and the event moves or disappears with the booking.
- **Client self-service:** clients reschedule or cancel from a link in their email, and returning clients get their own link with their remaining sessions.
- **Emails, in the business's own words:** confirmation, reschedule, cancellation, a reminder the day before, a notice to the business, and an optional rating request after the session.
- **Payments (optional):** full payment or a deposit at booking, taken on the business's own Stripe account. Refunds follow its cancellation rules.
- **Week view, client list, attendance and no-show marking.**
- **Reports:** visits by source, where people drop off, bookings, cancellation reasons and ratings.
- **In progress:** Outlook and Microsoft calendars, which the data model allows for but which aren't built. Automatic VAT at checkout, which is behind a setting that's off by default.

## 4. Problems it solves
- **Back-and-forth emails to find a time:** clients book themselves from live availability.
- **Wrong-fit enquiries and discovery calls:** the questions filter and route people before they take up a slot.
- **Double booking:** busy times come from Google Calendar.
- **No-shows and late changes:** the day-before reminder and one-click reschedule or cancel help, and paying or leaving a deposit at booking commits the client.
- **Losing interested people:** unfinished enquiries are kept and visible.
- **Guessing what works:** reports show where visitors come from and where they drop off.
- **A booking tool that feels foreign:** it can sit on the business's own site in its colours, with setup help in that website tool's own words.

## 5. Differentiators
Compared with a standard scheduler:
- the questions-first flow that routes people to an outcome;
- keeping and reporting unfinished enquiries;
- multi-session programmes with a client's session balance;
- no commission on client payments;
- AI-drafted questions and report summaries;
- website setup that detects the website tool and checks the code is working.

## 6. Pricing and plans
- A 7-day free trial. When it ends without a subscription, the account is locked; nothing is deleted.
- One paid plan, shown in the app as **€15 a month, VAT included**, billed monthly through Stripe. The amount actually charged is set in Stripe, not in the code.
- No commission on clients' payments.
- A "pro" plan name exists in the data model but has no features or price behind it.

## 7. Languages and markets
- **Language:** English only.
- **Currency:** euro by default; each business can choose its own.
- **Time zones:** any. Each business sets its own, and clients see the times converted to theirs.
- **Location:** run from Spain, under Spanish law and the Spanish data-protection authority.

## 8. Brand signals
- **Name:** always lowercase "khloe" in the interface.
- **Logo:** a wordmark in Arial Black, tightly spaced.
- **Colours:** Ink #202221, Warm Chalk #F4F1EB, Soft White #FCFBF8, Mist #D9D7D1, Graphite #666A67. Soft Mineral #566B68 means "where you are"; Ochre #B9822B means "needs you".
- **Fonts:** IBM Plex Sans for text and Arial for headlines. Pages use a limestone texture.
- **Tone:** plain, calm and second-person ("Your booking page", "Not seen yet"), with few exclamation marks and honest explanations of why something works the way it does.

## 9. Open questions for marketing
- The target segment to lead with (coaches, photographers or wedding planners), and the main markets beyond Spain.
- Whether a yearly plan or higher tiers are planned.
- When Outlook, team members and other languages are coming.
- Customer proof (testimonials, numbers): none exists in the product.
- The positioning line and the main competitor to compare against.
- When Google's calendar verification and live payments are expected to be complete.
