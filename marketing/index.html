The problem

A calendar can tell someone when a professional is free.
It cannot tell either side whether a meeting is worth having.

Core problem

In high-touch and expertise-led work, a 30–60 minute conversation is expensive in time and attention. A poor-fit call wastes capacity, creates awkward expectations, and often leaves both sides frustrated. Most booking tools begin with availability. Intro begins earlier — with the context needed to decide whether a conversation will be useful for both sides.

The framework

Understanding, alignment, then a worthwhile meeting.

01
Understanding

Gather context before availability.

Questions clarify what the person needs, what they expect, their readiness, and any criteria relevant to the professional — before a single time slot is shown.

02
Alignment

A decision about fit, not worthiness.

The professional's own criteria determine the appropriate next step. Alignment is mutual — it asks whether needs, timing and expectations are sufficiently matched for a conversation to be useful right now.

03
Meeting

The calendar is the outcome, not the start.

When alignment exists, availability opens. The meeting is what happens after understanding — not what a prospect has to get past to reach it.

Not every enquiry needs a meeting. Every enquiry deserves a useful next step.

The app protects the professional's time without making the prospective customer feel dismissed. It is discerning, but never superior. When a meeting isn't the right next step yet, the person receives a respectful alternative — not a rejection.

Product experience

Quiet for the professional. Almost invisible for the prospect.

The client-facing flow belongs visually to the professional — their logo, their accent colour, their name. Intro should barely announce itself there. The dashboard stays flat and quiet too: brand expression lives in onboarding and empty states, not in routine screens someone opens every day.

Who Intro is for

Any professional or service business where time starts with a conversation.

Not a narrow industry — the economics and human reality of expertise-led services, wherever the conversation has meaningful cost and context should shape whether availability is shown at all.
